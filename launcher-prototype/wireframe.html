1. Home, OBD connected

An OBD-II Bluetooth adapter (ELM327) plugged under the dashboard gives real speed, engine RPM, coolant temperature, fuel level and battery voltage.

1b. Home, no OBD (tablet GPS only)

Trip and drive time count from the tablet GPS. They reset on their own after the car has been still for 20 minutes, or at once with the reset button next to the GPS chip (tap and hold, so a stray touch can't clear a trip).

Sidebar: status at the top (tap it to open Controls), the 4 most recent apps with the open one marked, All apps at the bottom. A warning icon appears in the status only when the tablet is not charging or too hot.

1c. Car panel, GPS: heading as a compass

Sample heading 42° (north-east). The compass follows the GPS heading, so it only turns while the car moves (the tablet's GPS gives no heading when standing still: it keeps the last one).

A's four cards, with B's sliding tape inside the small Heading card: the heading (NE 42°) moves up beside the label and the tape takes the value's place, so the card stays the height of the others. The tape is about 120 px wide, so it is scaled to 1.3 px per degree: it shows about 90°, so the next main letter either side comes into view as you turn (here N on the left of NE).

App: the tape is drawn once as a strip of 0-360° (plus a wrap-around copy) and only shifted sideways (translateX) on each GPS update. Heading only changes while moving.

In the app all three cost the same: the dial, tape or ring is drawn once and only its rotation (or sideways shift) changes, on the existing once-a-second GPS update. Nothing animates in between.
